## The problem

Several hundred scheduled jobs ran on Kubernetes CronJobs, with two failures we
could not live with. Restarting a scheduling node fired jobs a second time, and
jobs had no dependency relationships, so downstream work started reading dirty
data before upstream had finished.

## What we did

A scheduler is stateful, so leader election has to be strongly consistent. There
is no shortcut available at that point.

- **Election** uses Raft (`hashicorp/raft`). Only the leader triggers work;
  followers just replicate state.
- **No duplicates** comes from writing an execution record, keyed on job plus
  trigger timestamp, into the Raft log. After a failover the new leader replays
  the log and knows exactly what has already fired.
- **No misses** comes from the leader rescanning, on startup, every job that
  should have fired within the window but has no record, then applying a
  catch-up policy to decide between running it late and skipping it.
- **Dependencies** are expressed as a DAG, topologically sorted and scheduled in
  layers. Cycles are rejected at submission time.

On timing, we avoided polling the whole table with a `time.Ticker` in favour of a
min-heap with a single timer set against the earliest job. CPU use barely moved
as the job count went from hundreds to tens of thousands.

## The outcome

- Failover completes in 1.2 seconds with no duplicate triggers — verified by a
  fortnight of chaos testing that killed the leader at random
- Scheduling precision holds at ±200ms
- Job count grew from 300 to 12,000 with scheduler CPU still under 5%
