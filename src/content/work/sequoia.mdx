## Why

Every layer of a database is described somewhere in a textbook, but the
textbook rarely shows where one layer hands off to the next. Sequoia is the
whole path in one small codebase: SQL text goes in at the top, 4 KB pages come
out at the bottom, and each layer uses only the ones below it. There are no
dependencies, so nothing is hidden behind a crate.

## What is in it

- **The language.** A hand-written lexer and recursive descent parser.
  Joins (inner, left, cross), `GROUP BY`/`HAVING`, `DISTINCT`, `ORDER BY`,
  `LIMIT`, and correlated, nested subqueries, with SQL's three-valued logic for
  `NULL`.
- **Planning.** `WHERE` and `ON` are split at their `AND`s and pushed down to
  the scan that can use them, where they pick an index. Each join is an index
  join, a hash join or a nested loop, in that order of preference. `EXPLAIN`
  prints the tree.
- **Execution.** Volcano style: every step is an iterator pulling rows from
  the one below, so a `LIMIT` stops the scan once it has enough. Sorting spills
  to an external merge sort past its memory budget, and becomes a top-N heap
  under a `LIMIT`.
- **Three storage engines.** A slotted-page heap, the default; an LSM tree with
  a skip-list MemTable, Bloom filters and level or universal compaction; and a
  PAX column store whose chunks pick their own encoding (run-length,
  bit-packing, delta, dictionary, bitmap) and are filtered while still
  compressed.
- **Indexes.** B+ trees with splits, merges and linked leaves, over one column
  of a heap or LSM table.
- **Durability.** A buffer pool with ARC replacement (LRU-K and CLOCK too), and
  a write-ahead log of checksummed page frames. Every statement is atomic,
  transactions have savepoints, and recovery keeps exactly the frames up to the
  last valid commit.

## How it is checked

Crash tests run every kind of statement on simulated files that stop at an
arbitrary write, then check that every table and index shows the statement
entirely or not at all. A differential test generates random schemas, rows,
queries and transactions, runs them on both Sequoia and SQLite, and fails —
printing the case as a script for the shell — the moment the two disagree.

## Status

Small and easy to read, not fast. One connection, so no concurrency control;
no `RIGHT` or `FULL` joins, no multi-column indexes.
